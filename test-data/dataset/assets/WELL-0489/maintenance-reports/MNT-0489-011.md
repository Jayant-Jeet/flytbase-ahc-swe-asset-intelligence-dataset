# Maintenance Report — MNT-0489-011

**Asset:** WELL-0489 (SE-32 Well 489, T200, Southeast Block 32)
**Equipment:** pump jack
**Work started:** 22 Mar 2024 08:00 · **Finished:** 09 Apr 2024 18:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 442 hours
**Crew lead:** Megan Ward (U-305) · **Crew:** Daniel Foster, Brian Coleman · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0489-0074 on 2024-03-21
**Report written:** 09 Apr 2024

## What happened

Field staff reported that the pumper heard grinding from the walking beam and saw the horsehead wobbling. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0489-0074 showed the wellhead at 20.4 °C with the air at 16.8 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. A vibration check and visual inspection found play in the saddle bearing and scoring on the bearing housing.
The well stayed shut in for about 16 days longer than the repair itself because the replacement part was back-ordered from the supplier.

## Root cause

Grease contaminated with sand had worn the bearing.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 2 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Lowered the horsehead and supported the walking beam with a crane.
2. Removed the saddle bearing assembly.
3. Cleaned the housing and checked alignment.
4. Fitted a new saddle bearing and torqued the bolts to the manufacturer's values.
5. Greased all structural bearings.

Summary of the fix: replaced saddle bearing and re-greased.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the unit for 2 hours: smooth stroke, no knocking, bearing temperature normal. The well was returned to production.
