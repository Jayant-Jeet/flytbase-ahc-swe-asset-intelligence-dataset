# Maintenance Report — MNT-0427-020

**Asset:** WELL-0427 (NW-12 Well 427, T200, Northwest Block 12)
**Equipment:** downhole pump
**Work started:** 14 Feb 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 18 Feb 2026 20:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 108 hours
**Crew lead:** Daniel Foster (U-302) · **Crew:** Megan Ward
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0427-0172 on 2026-02-12
**Report written:** 19 Feb 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that the wellhead was cold and the pump jack was straining with a heavy, uneven load. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0427-0172 showed the wellhead at 9.7 °C with the air at 10.5 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. The downhole pump was stuck, most likely from sand or scale.
The company workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 2 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Free gas entering the pump stopped it from filling, and the dry plunger seized.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 8 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

**Parts installed:** Highline RP-175 pump, serial SN-529288, lot LOT-2409-C

## Work done

1. Rigged up the company workover rig on the well.
2. Pulled the rods and the insert pump.
3. Inspected the pump: sand in the barrel and a worn plunger.
4. Ran in a new insert pump.
5. Re-spaced the pump and hung the rods back on.

Summary of the fix: pulled and replaced downhole pump.

## Testing and return to service

Pump test confirmed fluid to surface and the wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
