# Maintenance Report — MNT-0369-010

**Asset:** WELL-0369 (NW-12 Well 369, T200, Northwest Block 12)
**Equipment:** pump jack
**Work started:** 16 Mar 2024 08:00 · **Finished:** 26 Mar 2024 18:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 250 hours
**Crew lead:** Daniel Foster (U-302) · **Crew:** Megan Ward · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0369-0073 on 2024-03-14
**Report written:** 27 Mar 2024

## What happened

Field staff reported that the pump jack was running rough with a loud knock at the top of each stroke, so the operator stopped it. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0369-0073 showed the wellhead at 14.5 °C with the air at 11.7 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. A vibration check and visual inspection found play in the saddle bearing and scoring on the bearing housing.
The well stayed shut in for about 8 days longer than the repair itself because the job had to wait for a weekday contractor slot.

## Root cause

Bearing wear from long service and an overdue grease interval.
This is corrective repair number 3 on this well's pump jack in the last 12 months.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started, but it was not written down (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 0 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Lowered the horsehead and supported the walking beam with a crane.
2. Removed the saddle bearing assembly.
3. Cleaned the housing and checked alignment.
4. Fitted a new saddle bearing and torqued the bolts to the manufacturer's values.
5. Greased all structural bearings.

Summary of the fix: replaced saddle bearing and re-greased.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the unit for 2 hours: smooth stroke, no knocking, bearing temperature normal. The well was returned to production.
