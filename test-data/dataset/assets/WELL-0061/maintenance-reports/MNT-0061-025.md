# Maintenance Report — MNT-0061-025

**Asset:** WELL-0061 (SW-24 Well 061, T200, Southwest Block 24)
**Equipment:** rod string
**Work started:** 06 Mar 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 14 Apr 2026 04:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 932 hours
**Crew lead:** Daniel Foster (U-302) · **Crew:** Brian Coleman · **Contractor:** Sandstone Rod & Pump
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0061-0176 on 2026-03-05
**Report written:** 15 Apr 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that the operator noticed the pump jack running unusually light and no fluid reaching the flowline. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0061-0176 showed the wellhead at 14.5 °C with the air at 11.2 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. A dynamometer card showed no load from the downhole pump, which points to a parted rod.
Sandstone Rod & Pump's workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 37 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Fatigue failure of a rod body, probably started by corrosion pitting.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 2 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the company workover rig on the well.
2. Pulled the rod string and located the break.
3. Recovered the lower section with an overshot fishing tool.
4. Replaced the damaged rods.
5. Ran the rod string back in and spaced out the pump.

Summary of the fix: fished and replaced parted rod section.

## Testing and return to service

Pump test showed normal fluid delivery and the rod load returned to normal. The well was returned to production.
