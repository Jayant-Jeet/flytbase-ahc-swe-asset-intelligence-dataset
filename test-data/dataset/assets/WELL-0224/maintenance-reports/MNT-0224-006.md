# Maintenance Report — MNT-0224-006

**Asset:** WELL-0224 (SE-31 Well 224, T350, Southeast Block 31)
**Equipment:** pump jack
**Work started:** 08 Sep 2023 08:00 · **Finished:** 26 Sep 2023 17:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 441 hours
**Crew lead:** Megan Ward (U-305) · **Crew:** Daniel Foster · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0224-0047 on 2023-09-07
**Report written:** 28 Sep 2023

## What happened

Field staff reported that oil was dripping from the gearbox onto the pump jack base. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0224-0047 showed the wellhead at 42.6 °C with the air at 31.9 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. The input shaft seal was worn and the gearbox oil level was below the sight glass.
The well stayed shut in for about 16 days longer than the repair itself because the crew was committed to a higher-priority spill response elsewhere in the field.

## Root cause

A hardened output shaft seal after years of service.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 1 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Drained the gearbox oil into a closed container.
2. Replaced the input and output shaft seals.
3. Cleaned the base and absorbed the spilled oil.
4. Refilled with the specified gear oil to the correct level.

Summary of the fix: replaced gearbox seals and topped up oil.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the unit for 1 hour and checked both shafts for leaks: none found. The well was returned to production.

## Waste

- Used gear oil and oily pads sent to an approved disposal site under waste manifest WM-0224-006 (WS-06).
