# Maintenance Report — MNT-0323-005

**Asset:** WELL-0323 (NW-12 Well 323, T200, Northwest Block 12)
**Equipment:** downhole pump
**Work started:** 25 Aug 2023 08:00 · **Finished:** 29 Aug 2023 13:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 101 hours
**Crew lead:** Kevin Price (U-303) · **Crew:** Megan Ward, Brian Coleman
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0323-0045 on 2023-08-24
**Report written:** 29 Aug 2023

## What happened

Field staff reported that fluid stopped reaching the flowline and the rod load spiked on the downstroke. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0323-0045 showed the wellhead at 42.6 °C with the air at 36.7 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. The downhole pump was stuck, most likely from sand or scale.
The company workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 2 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

A worn plunger that let sand settle and lock the pump.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 2 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the company workover rig on the well.
2. Pulled the rods and the insert pump.
3. Inspected the pump: sand in the barrel and a worn plunger.
4. Ran in a new insert pump.
5. Re-spaced the pump and hung the rods back on.

Summary of the fix: pulled and replaced downhole pump.

## Testing and return to service

Pump test confirmed fluid to surface and the wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
