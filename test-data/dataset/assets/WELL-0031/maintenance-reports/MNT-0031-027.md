# Maintenance Report — MNT-0031-027

**Asset:** WELL-0031 (SW-23 Well 031, T200, Southwest Block 23)
**Equipment:** downhole pump
**Work started:** 24 Apr 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 06 May 2026 20:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 300 hours
**Crew lead:** Brian Coleman (U-304) · **Crew:** Daniel Foster, Kevin Price · **Contractor:** Sandstone Rod & Pump
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0031-0177 on 2026-04-23
**Report written:** 08 May 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that the wellhead was cold and the pump jack was straining with a heavy, uneven load. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0031-0177 showed the wellhead at 26.0 °C with the air at 25.5 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. The downhole pump was stuck, most likely from sand or scale.
Sandstone Rod & Pump's workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 10 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

A worn plunger that let sand settle and lock the pump.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 4 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

**Parts installed:** Highline RP-175 pump, serial SN-664877, lot LOT-2409-A

## Work done

1. Rigged up the company workover rig on the well.
2. Pulled the rods and the insert pump.
3. Inspected the pump: sand in the barrel and a worn plunger.
4. Ran in a new insert pump.
5. Re-spaced the pump and hung the rods back on.

Summary of the fix: pulled and replaced downhole pump.

## Testing and return to service

Pump test confirmed fluid to surface and the wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
