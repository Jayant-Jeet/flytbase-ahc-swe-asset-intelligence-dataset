# Maintenance Report — MNT-0121-031

**Asset:** WELL-0121 (NE-07 Well 121, T200, Northeast Block 7)
**Equipment:** pump jack
**Work started:** 30 May 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 23 Jun 2026 06:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 574 hours
**Crew lead:** Brian Coleman (U-304) · **Crew:** Kevin Price, Daniel Foster · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0121-0186 on 2026-05-28
**Report written:** 24 Jun 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that oil was dripping from the gearbox onto the pump jack base. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0121-0186 showed the wellhead at 34.9 °C with the air at 35.2 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. The input shaft seal was worn and the gearbox oil level was below the sight glass.
The well stayed shut in for about 22 days longer than the repair itself because the replacement part was back-ordered from the supplier.

## Root cause

Seal wear, made worse by heat and a low oil level.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 1 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Drained the gearbox oil into a closed container.
2. Replaced the input and output shaft seals.
3. Cleaned the base and absorbed the spilled oil.
4. Refilled with the specified gear oil to the correct level.

Summary of the fix: replaced gearbox seals and topped up oil.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the unit for 1 hour and checked both shafts for leaks: none found. The well was returned to production.

## Waste

- Used gear oil and oily pads sent to an approved disposal site under waste manifest WM-0121-031 (WS-06).
