# Maintenance Report — MNT-0027-006

**Asset:** WELL-0027 (SW-23 Well 027, T200, Southwest Block 23)
**Equipment:** rod string
**Work started:** 19 Nov 2023 08:00 · **Finished:** 29 Nov 2023 22:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 254 hours
**Crew lead:** Megan Ward (U-305) · **Crew:** Kevin Price, Brian Coleman · **Contractor:** Sandstone Rod & Pump
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0027-0055 on 2023-11-16
**Report written:** 29 Nov 2023

## What happened

Field staff reported that the operator noticed the pump jack running unusually light and no fluid reaching the flowline. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0027-0055 showed the wellhead at 21.8 °C with the air at 17.3 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. A dynamometer card showed no load from the downhole pump, which points to a parted rod.
Sandstone Rod & Pump's workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 9 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Fatigue failure of a rod body, probably started by corrosion pitting.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 2 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the company workover rig on the well.
2. Pulled the rod string and located the break.
3. Recovered the lower section with an overshot fishing tool.
4. Replaced the damaged rods.
5. Ran the rod string back in and spaced out the pump.

Summary of the fix: fished and replaced parted rod section.

## Testing and return to service

Pump test showed normal fluid delivery and the rod load returned to normal. The well was returned to production.
