# Maintenance Report — MNT-0248-015

**Asset:** WELL-0248 (SE-31 Well 248, T500, Southeast Block 31)
**Equipment:** flowline
**Work started:** 08 Nov 2024 08:00 · **Finished:** 18 Dec 2024 04:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 956 hours
**Crew lead:** Daniel Foster (U-302) · **Crew:** Kevin Price
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0248-0103 on 2024-11-07
**Report written:** 19 Dec 2024

## What happened

Field staff reported that the inspector saw wet soil above the buried flowline. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0248-0103 showed the wellhead at 35.5 °C with the air at 20.4 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. Excavation found a corrosion pinhole on the bottom of the line.
The well stayed shut in for about 38 days longer than the repair itself because heavy rain made the lease road impassable for the service truck.

## Root cause

External corrosion where the pipe coating had been damaged.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: ESP drive stopped, drive disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 1 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).
- Hot work permit issued for welding; gas test before welding read 1% LEL, below the 10% limit (WS-04).

## Work done

1. Excavated around the leak.
2. Cut out the corroded section.
3. Welded in a new section of line pipe.
4. Coated the new joint.
5. Backfilled after the pressure test.

Summary of the fix: cut out and replaced the corroded flowline section.

## Testing and return to service

Pressure-tested the repaired section at 229 psi (1.25 × the flowline's maximum operating pressure of 183 psi) and held it for 4 hours with no pressure drop (WS-05). The well was returned to production.

## Waste

- Oily soil from the excavation and the cut-out pipe sent to an approved disposal site under waste manifest WM-0248-015 (WS-06).
