# Maintenance Report — MNT-0326-025

**Asset:** WELL-0326 (NW-12 Well 326, T350, Northwest Block 12)
**Equipment:** pump jack
**Work started:** 18 Sep 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 14 Oct 2026 10:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 626 hours
**Crew lead:** Kevin Price (U-303) · **Crew:** Brian Coleman, Megan Ward · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0326-0204 on 2026-09-17
**Report written:** 14 Oct 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that the pumper heard grinding from the walking beam and saw the horsehead wobbling. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0326-0204 showed the wellhead at 39.6 °C with the air at 29.5 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. A vibration check and visual inspection found play in the saddle bearing and scoring on the bearing housing.
The well stayed shut in for about 24 days longer than the repair itself because heavy rain made the lease road impassable for the service truck.

## Root cause

Bearing wear from long service and an overdue grease interval.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 6 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Lowered the horsehead and supported the walking beam with a crane.
2. Removed the saddle bearing assembly.
3. Cleaned the housing and checked alignment.
4. Fitted a new saddle bearing and torqued the bolts to the manufacturer's values.
5. Greased all structural bearings.

Summary of the fix: replaced saddle bearing and re-greased.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the unit for 2 hours: smooth stroke, no knocking, bearing temperature normal. The well was returned to production.
