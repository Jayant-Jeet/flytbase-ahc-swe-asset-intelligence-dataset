# Maintenance Report — MNT-0578-006

**Asset:** WELL-0578 (SE-32 Well 578, T500, Southeast Block 32)
**Equipment:** esp pump
**Work started:** 02 Sep 2023 08:00 · **Finished:** 12 Sep 2023 11:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 243 hours
**Crew lead:** Megan Ward (U-305) · **Crew:** Daniel Foster · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0578-0046 on 2023-08-31
**Report written:** 13 Sep 2023

## What happened

Field staff reported that the ESP drive tripped on motor overcurrent and would not restart. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0578-0046 showed the wellhead at 34.9 °C with the air at 30.2 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. An insulation-resistance test from surface showed the downhole ESP motor windings had failed.
Coyote Ridge Well Services's workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 8 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Motor insulation breakdown with age and heat.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: ESP drive stopped, drive disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 4 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the workover rig and pulled the tubing with the ESP string.
2. Laid down the failed motor and inspected the pump and seal sections.
3. Made up a new motor and tested it on surface.
4. Ran the ESP string back in and landed the tubing.
5. Restarted the drive and tuned the frequency.

Summary of the fix: pulled the ESP and replaced the motor.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the ESP for 4 hours: stable motor current, fluid to surface, wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
