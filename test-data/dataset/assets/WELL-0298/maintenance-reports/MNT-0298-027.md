# Maintenance Report — MNT-0298-027

**Asset:** WELL-0298 (NE-08 Well 298, T350, Northeast Block 8)
**Equipment:** pump jack
**Work started:** 22 Mar 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 07 Apr 2026 08:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 384 hours
**Crew lead:** Megan Ward (U-305) · **Crew:** Kevin Price · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0298-0174 on 2026-03-19
**Report written:** 08 Apr 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that the pump jack was running rough with a loud knock at the top of each stroke, so the operator stopped it. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0298-0174 showed the wellhead at 21.3 °C with the air at 18.4 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. A vibration check and visual inspection found play in the saddle bearing and scoring on the bearing housing.
The well stayed shut in for about 14 days longer than the repair itself because the crew was committed to a higher-priority spill response elsewhere in the field.

## Root cause

Grease contaminated with sand had worn the bearing.
This is corrective repair number 3 on this well's pump jack in the last 12 months.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 2 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Lowered the horsehead and supported the walking beam with a crane.
2. Removed the saddle bearing assembly.
3. Cleaned the housing and checked alignment.
4. Fitted a new saddle bearing and torqued the bolts to the manufacturer's values.
5. Greased all structural bearings.

Summary of the fix: replaced saddle bearing and re-greased.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the unit for 2 hours: smooth stroke, no knocking, bearing temperature normal. The well was returned to production.
