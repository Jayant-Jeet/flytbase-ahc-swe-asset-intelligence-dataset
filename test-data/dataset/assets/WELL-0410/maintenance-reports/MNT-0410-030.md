# Maintenance Report — MNT-0410-030

**Asset:** WELL-0410 (NW-12 Well 410, T500, Northwest Block 12)
**Equipment:** esp pump
**Work started:** 05 Dec 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 30 Dec 2026 12:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 604 hours
**Crew lead:** Brian Coleman (U-304) · **Crew:** Kevin Price
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0410-0212 on 2026-12-03
**Report written:** 30 Dec 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that the ESP drive tripped on motor overcurrent and would not restart. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0410-0212 showed the wellhead at 11.9 °C with the air at 8.5 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. An insulation-resistance test from surface showed the downhole ESP motor windings had failed.
The company workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 23 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Gas lock in the pump starved the motor of cooling fluid.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: ESP drive stopped, drive disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 1 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the workover rig and pulled the tubing with the ESP string.
2. Laid down the failed motor and inspected the pump and seal sections.
3. Made up a new motor and tested it on surface.
4. Ran the ESP string back in and landed the tubing.
5. Restarted the drive and tuned the frequency.

Summary of the fix: pulled the ESP and replaced the motor.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the ESP for 4 hours: stable motor current, fluid to surface, wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
