# Maintenance Report — MNT-0055-022

**Asset:** WELL-0055 (SW-24 Well 055, T350, Southwest Block 24)
**Equipment:** pump jack
**Work started:** 07 Nov 2025 08:00 · **Finished:** 01 Dec 2025 23:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 591 hours
**Crew lead:** Megan Ward (U-305) · **Crew:** Brian Coleman
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0055-0157 on 2025-11-06
**Report written:** 02 Dec 2025

## What happened

Field staff reported that oil was dripping from the gearbox onto the pump jack base. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0055-0157 showed the wellhead at 15.9 °C with the air at 16.2 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. The input shaft seal was worn and the gearbox oil level was below the sight glass.
The well stayed shut in for about 23 days longer than the repair itself because the crew was committed to a higher-priority spill response elsewhere in the field.

## Root cause

A hardened output shaft seal after years of service.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 1 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Drained the gearbox oil into a closed container.
2. Replaced the input and output shaft seals.
3. Cleaned the base and absorbed the spilled oil.
4. Refilled with the specified gear oil to the correct level.

Summary of the fix: replaced gearbox seals and topped up oil.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the unit for 1 hour and checked both shafts for leaks: none found. The well was returned to production.

## Waste

- Used gear oil and oily pads sent to an approved disposal site under waste manifest WM-0055-022 (WS-06).
