# Maintenance Report — MNT-0426-005

**Asset:** WELL-0426 (NW-12 Well 426, T350, Northwest Block 12)
**Equipment:** downhole pump
**Work started:** 23 Apr 2023 08:00 · **Finished:** 10 May 2023 00:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 400 hours
**Crew lead:** Brian Coleman (U-304) · **Crew:** Kevin Price, Megan Ward
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0426-0027 on 2023-04-20
**Report written:** 10 May 2023

## What happened

Field staff reported that the wellhead was cold and the pump jack was straining with a heavy, uneven load. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0426-0027 showed the wellhead at 25.0 °C with the air at 24.1 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. The downhole pump was stuck, most likely from sand or scale.
The company workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 15 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Sand and scale build-up in the pump barrel.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 5 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the company workover rig on the well.
2. Pulled the rods and the insert pump.
3. Inspected the pump: sand in the barrel and a worn plunger.
4. Ran in a new insert pump.
5. Re-spaced the pump and hung the rods back on.

Summary of the fix: pulled and replaced downhole pump.

## Testing and return to service

Pump test confirmed fluid to surface and the wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
