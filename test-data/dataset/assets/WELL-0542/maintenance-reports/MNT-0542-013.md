# Maintenance Report — MNT-0542-013

**Asset:** WELL-0542 (SE-32 Well 542, T500, Southeast Block 32)
**Equipment:** esp pump
**Work started:** 15 Dec 2023 08:00 · **Finished:** 08 Jan 2024 22:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 590 hours
**Crew lead:** Kevin Price (U-303) · **Crew:** Brian Coleman, Daniel Foster · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0542-0061 on 2023-12-14
**Report written:** 09 Jan 2024

## What happened

Field staff reported that the ESP drive tripped on motor overcurrent and would not restart. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0542-0061 showed the wellhead at 16.7 °C with the air at 12.4 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. An insulation-resistance test from surface showed the downhole ESP motor windings had failed.
Coyote Ridge Well Services's workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 23 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Motor overheating after weeks of running with low fluid over the pump.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: ESP drive stopped, drive disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 0 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the workover rig and pulled the tubing with the ESP string.
2. Laid down the failed motor and inspected the pump and seal sections.
3. Made up a new motor and tested it on surface.
4. Ran the ESP string back in and landed the tubing.
5. Restarted the drive and tuned the frequency.

Summary of the fix: pulled the ESP and replaced the motor.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the ESP for 4 hours: stable motor current, fluid to surface, wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
