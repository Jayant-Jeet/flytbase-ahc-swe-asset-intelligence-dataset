# Maintenance Report — MNT-0588-022

**Asset:** WELL-0588 (SE-32 Well 588, T500, Southeast Block 32)
**Equipment:** esp pump
**Work started:** 08 May 2026 08:00 · **Finished:** 15 Jun 2026 20:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 924 hours
**Crew lead:** Daniel Foster (U-302) · **Crew:** Megan Ward, Brian Coleman
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0588-0185 on 2026-05-07
**Report written:** 16 Jun 2026

## What happened

Field staff reported that the drive showed falling motor current and then a ground fault. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0588-0185 showed the wellhead at 36.1 °C with the air at 23.8 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. An insulation-resistance test from surface showed the downhole ESP motor windings had failed.
The company workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 36 days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Motor overheating after weeks of running with low fluid over the pump.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: ESP drive stopped, drive disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 4 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the workover rig and pulled the tubing with the ESP string.
2. Laid down the failed motor and inspected the pump and seal sections.
3. Made up a new motor and tested it on surface.
4. Ran the ESP string back in and landed the tubing.
5. Restarted the drive and tuned the frequency.

Summary of the fix: pulled the ESP and replaced the motor.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the ESP for 4 hours: stable motor current, fluid to surface, wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
