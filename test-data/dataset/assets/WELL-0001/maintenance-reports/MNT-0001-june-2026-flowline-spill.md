# Maintenance Report — MNT-0001-029

**Asset:** WELL-0001 (SW-23 Well 001, T200, Southwest Block 23)
**Equipment:** flowline
**Work started:** 12 Jun 2026 07:00 · **Finished:** 13 Jun 2026 18:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 35 hours
**Crew lead:** Daniel Foster (U-302) · **Crew:** Brian Coleman, Megan Ward · **Contractor:** Tumbleweed Flowline Co.
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0001-0185 on 2026-06-11
**Report written:** 15 Jun 2026

## What happened

The weekly drone inspection INS-0001-0185 at 14:30 on 11 June 2026 found crude oil pooled
around the flowline coupling about 6 m downstream of the wellhead. Roughly 9.4% of the pad
was stained, an estimated 6.8 bbl. That is a reportable spill under the Spill Response SOP,
and because it covered more than 5% of the pad the T200 manual requires an immediate
shut-in. The field operator, Jake Morales, shut the well in that afternoon. The crew on
call was finishing another job, so earth berms and absorbent pads were only in place in the
evening: the release was **contained about 7 hours after the inspection flight**.

The spill report to the State Oil & Gas Commission was filed 41 hours after detection, on
13 June. The HSE on-call handover had been missed, so nobody owned the report on 11–12 June.

## Root cause

The threaded coupling had external corrosion under a failed protective wrap. This is the
second flowline failure in a month on this well: a pinhole leak was repaired on 10 May 2026
(MNT-0001-028). The flowline passed its annual integrity test on 7 March 2026
(MNT-0001-026). A pressure test does not reveal wall loss hidden under a wrap, so the
weakness was missed.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew and the contractor before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: prime mover stopped at the bottom of the stroke, brake set and chained, motor disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve and flowline valve closed and tagged, line pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 2 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).
- Hot work permit issued for welding; gas test before welding read 0% LEL (WS-04).

## Work done

1. Removed free oil from the pad with a vacuum truck and absorbent pads.
2. Excavated around the coupling and exposed 6 m of flowline.
3. Cut out the failed coupling and 4 m of corroded pipe.
4. Welded in new pipe and a new coupling, then wrapped the joint.
5. Excavated the stained soil around the coupling and across the pad.

Summary of the fix: replaced coupling and 4 m of flowline; excavated and bagged contaminated soil.

## Testing and return to service

Pressure-tested the repaired section at 196 psi (1.25 × the flowline's maximum operating
pressure of 157 psi) and held for 4 hours with no pressure drop (WS-05). The well was returned to
production at 18:00 on 13 June.

## Spill clean-up and waste

- About 3.3 m³ of stained soil excavated, bagged and shipped to an approved disposal site under waste manifest WM-0001-029 (WS-06).
- 4 soil samples taken around the spill; the highest result after clean-up was 0.8% TPH, below the 1.0% target. Agency verification requested (SR-03).

## Notes and recommendations

- HSE to fix the on-call handover so reportable spills always have an owner.
- Replace the whole flowline on this well instead of patching sections; two failures in a month show the line is in poor condition.
- Add a visual check of coupling wraps to the flowline inspection, because pressure tests miss wall loss under a wrap.
