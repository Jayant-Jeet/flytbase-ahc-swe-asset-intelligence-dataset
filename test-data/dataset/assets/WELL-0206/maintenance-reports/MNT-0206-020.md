# Maintenance Report — MNT-0206-020

**Asset:** WELL-0206 (SE-31 Well 206, T500, Southeast Block 31)
**Equipment:** esp pump
**Work started:** 03 May 2025 08:00 · **Finished:** 06 May 2025 11:00 · **Well stopped for this job:** 75 hours
**Crew lead:** Brian Coleman (U-304) · **Crew:** Kevin Price · **Contractor:** Coyote Ridge Well Services
**Triggered by:** inspection INS-0206-0132 on 2025-05-01
**Report written:** 07 May 2025

## What happened

Field staff reported that the ESP drive tripped on motor overcurrent and would not restart. The thermal reading at inspection INS-0206-0132 showed the wellhead at 24.1 °C with the air at 24.4 °C, so the well was not producing. The operator shut the well in for the repair. An insulation-resistance test from surface showed the downhole ESP motor windings had failed.
Coyote Ridge Well Services's workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about 1 day for it; the work itself took about 2 days.

## Root cause

Motor overheating after weeks of running with low fluid over the pump.
This is corrective repair number 4 on this well's esp pump in the last 12 months.

## Safety and permits

- Pre-job safety meeting held with the crew before work started (WS-01).
- Energy isolated: ESP drive stopped, drive disconnect locked and tagged, wellhead wing valve closed and tagged, pressure bled off (WS-02).
- Personal H2S monitors worn; highest reading 0 ppm, below the 10 ppm stop-work level (WS-03).

## Work done

1. Rigged up the workover rig and pulled the tubing with the ESP string.
2. Laid down the failed motor and inspected the pump and seal sections.
3. Made up a new motor and tested it on surface.
4. Ran the ESP string back in and landed the tubing.
5. Restarted the drive and tuned the frequency.

Summary of the fix: pulled the ESP and replaced the motor.

## Testing and return to service

Ran the ESP for 4 hours: stable motor current, fluid to surface, wellhead warmed back to normal. The well was returned to production.
