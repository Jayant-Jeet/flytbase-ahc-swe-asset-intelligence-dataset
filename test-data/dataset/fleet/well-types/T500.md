# T500 Electric Submersible Pump Oil Well — Operating & Maintenance Manual

> Fictional manual written for the FlytBase Asset Copilot hackathon. Modelled on typical
> electric submersible pump (ESP) well practice. Not for real-world operational use.

Document ID: OM-T500 · Revision 1 · Applies to all wells with `well_type = "T500"`

## 1. Scope

The T500 is a high-rate well lifted by an **electric submersible pump (ESP)** instead of a
pump jack and rod string. There is no surface pumping unit: a variable-speed drive on the
pad powers a motor and multistage pump hung on the tubing deep in the well. The wellhead,
flowline and optional separator are the same families as on rod-lift wells.

## 2. Equipment overview

| Equipment | Typical model | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Wellhead | WH-5000 | Seals the well, carries the power cable penetrator and production valves |
| ESP (`esp_pump`) | ESP-400 | Downhole motor, seal section and multistage pump, with its surface drive and power cable |
| Flowline | 3 in carbon steel | Carries produced fluid to the gathering line |
| Separator (optional) | 2-phase vertical 24 in | Separates gas from liquid on the pad |

A T500 pad has **no pump jack, rod string or rod pump**. There is no visible stroke, so a
drone cannot tell from movement alone whether the well is running.

## 3. Production temperature rule

ESP wells move more fluid, and the motor adds heat. A T500 well is considered
**producing (active)** only when its wellhead temperature is at least **25 °C above
ambient** air temperature at the time of the reading. Below 25 °C the well is **not
producing** for that reading.

## 4. Temperature alarm

The wellhead must stay **at or below 95 °C**. Above 95 °C, shut in and check the drive
and motor within 24 hours.

## 5. Early warning signs

An ESP runs at a steady speed, so a healthy T500 wellhead stays within a degree or two of
its usual difference above air. ESP motors usually fail after running hot with too little
fluid over the pump. In the weeks before a failure, the wellhead-to-air difference typically
**falls steadily** while the well is still producing.

- If the wellhead-to-air difference is **lower than at the inspection before, three
  inspections in a row** (four readings, each lower than the last) **and has fallen by
  3 °C or more in total** over those four readings, schedule an ESP drive and insulation
  check within **14 days** of the third drop.
- A ground fault or repeated trips on the drive must be treated as a failure, not reset.

## 6. Spill-related shut-in

Same as T200: a spill covering more than 5 % of the pad area requires immediate shut-in.

## 7. Maintenance escalation

- **Two or more** corrective maintenance events on the **ESP** in the 12 months before the
  current date require a root-cause analysis (RCA) before the next repair is closed.
  (ESP pulls need a workover rig and cost far more than rod-lift repairs.)
- For all other equipment, follow T200 section 10 (three events in 12 months).
- Downtime of more than 14 consecutive days must be reported to the area production supervisor.
- An annual flowline integrity test is mandatory (see Regulatory Compliance SOP, RC-02).
