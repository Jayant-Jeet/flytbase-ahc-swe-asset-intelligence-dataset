# T200 Rod-Lift Oil Well — Operating & Maintenance Manual

> Fictional manual written for the FlytBase Asset Copilot hackathon. Modelled on typical
> sucker-rod (beam pump) well practice. Not for real-world operational use.

Document ID: OM-T200 · Revision 4 · Applies to all wells with `well_type = "T200"`

## 1. Scope

This manual covers the T200 class of onshore rod-lift (beam pump) oil wells: a
conventional wellhead, a surface pump jack (walking-beam pumping unit), a sucker
rod string, an insert downhole pump and a 3-inch carbon-steel flowline to the
area gathering line. Some T200 pads also carry a two-phase vertical separator.

## 2. Equipment overview

| Equipment | Typical model | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Wellhead | WH-3000 | Seals the well, holds the stuffing box and production valves |
| Pump jack | PJ-228 | Converts motor rotation to reciprocating rod motion |
| Rod string | API Grade D 7/8 in | Transmits motion from the pump jack to the downhole pump |
| Downhole pump | Insert rod pump 1.75 in | Lifts fluid from the reservoir |
| Flowline | 3 in carbon steel | Carries produced fluid to the gathering line |
| Separator (optional) | 2-phase vertical 24 in | Separates gas from liquid on the pad |

## 3. Normal operation

A producing T200 well cycles continuously at 6–9 strokes per minute. Produced
fluid leaves the reservoir warmer than the surface air, so a producing wellhead
runs noticeably warmer than ambient. When the pump stops, the
wellhead cools to near ambient within a few hours.

Operators should expect daily and seasonal variation. On hot summer afternoons
the steel of an idle wellhead can read well above 45 °C from solar heating alone.
**Never judge production from absolute wellhead temperature.**

## 4. Start-up and shut-in

1. Confirm the flowline valve is open and the separator (if fitted) is at operating level.
2. Release the pump jack brake and start the prime mover.
3. Confirm stroke rate within 6–9 spm and check the stuffing box for leaks.
4. For a planned shut-in, stop the prime mover at the bottom of the stroke, set the brake,
   close the wing valve and record the shut-in in the maintenance system before work starts.

## 5. Routine inspection checklist

- Visual check of pad, cellar and flowline route for staining or pooled fluid.
- Thermal reading of the wellhead (drone or handheld).
- Stuffing box leakage, belt condition and gearbox oil level.
- Separator level and dump valve operation (if fitted).

## 6. Lubrication schedule

| Item | Interval |
|---|---|
| Saddle and tail bearings | 90 days |
| Gearbox oil level check | 30 days |
| Gearbox oil change | 12 months |
| Belt tension | 60 days |

## 7. Common failure modes

| Symptom | Likely cause | First action |
|---|---|---|
| Unit running, wellhead cold | Rod parted or pump stuck | Shut in, call rod/pump crew |
| Unit stopped, motor hot | Prime mover overload | Reset overload, check cooling |
| Oil on gearbox base | Seal failure | Replace seals |
| Staining along flowline | Corrosion pinhole or coupling failure | Shut in, contain, report per SOP SR |
| Stuffing box weeping | Worn packing | Replace packing |

## 8. Spill response on the pad

Any crude release must be handled under the company Spill Response SOP
(`sops/spill-response-sop.md`). In short: stop the source, contain, estimate the
volume, and report within the deadline set by the SOP.

## 9. Engineering limits

### 9.1 Pressure
Maximum allowable wellhead working pressure: 3,000 psi.

### 9.2 Stroke
Maximum stroke rate: 10 spm. Maximum polished-rod load: 22,800 lb.

### 9.3 Production temperature rule
A T200 well is considered **producing (active)** when its wellhead temperature is
at least **15 °C above ambient** air temperature at the time of the reading. A
difference below 15 °C means the well is **not producing** for that reading,
whatever the absolute temperature.

### 9.4 Temperature alarm
The wellhead must stay **at or below 85 °C**. Any reading above 85 °C requires the
operator to shut in and inspect the stuffing box and rod string within 24 hours.

### 9.5 Spill-related shut-in
If a spill covers **more than 5 % of the pad area**, shut in the well immediately and
keep it shut in until the source is repaired.

## 10. Maintenance escalation

- Three or more **corrective** maintenance events on the same equipment kind in the
  12 months before the current date require a root-cause analysis (RCA) before the next repair is closed.
- Downtime of more than 14 consecutive days must be reported to the area production supervisor.
- An annual flowline integrity test is mandatory (see Regulatory Compliance SOP, RC-02).

## 11. Records

Every inspection, maintenance job and shut-in must be recorded. Human notes that
explain an abnormal reading (for example a planned shut-in) are valid context and
should be considered alongside the sensor data.
